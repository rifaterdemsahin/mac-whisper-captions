Why Live Captions Aren't Always Enough

The "Baked-In" Problem

While this project successfully provides ultra-low latency, highly accurate subtitles for live streaming via OBS, it suffers from a fundamental flaw when it comes to recorded content creation.

If you are recording a video with OBS and broadcasting these live captions over your footage, those words become permanently "baked" into the video pixels.

"When wrong baked words are inside the footage, we cannot edit it out. For that reason, this project is not useful for consistency."

Even with the best AI models in the world, Whisper will occasionally hallucinate, misspell a name, or misinterpret a sentence. If this happens during a live stream, it's accepted. But if you are recording a video for YouTube, a single bad transcription ruins the clip because you cannot fix it in post-production without blurring or cropping the footage.

The Solution: Post-Production Editors

For consistent, high-quality, and professional video production, live baked-in subtitles are not the answer. You need to record the raw, clean footage without any overlays, and then use a non-linear editor that can read, transcribe, and allow you to manually update the text.

Tools like Canva, CapCut, or Premiere Pro are required for this workflow because they treat subtitles as an editable text layer, rather than burnt-in pixels.
